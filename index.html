Privacy Policy

Crisis Lines · last updated 23 August 2026

The app keeps one thing: the county you chose. It stays on your phone.

You can clear it any time by tapping Change. There is no account and no sign-in. Nothing you do in this app is sent to us, because there is no server on the other end to send it to.

This app is a directory of mental health and crisis services. It is a list of phone numbers and addresses. It is not a screening tool, it does not assess anyone, and it never asks how you are feeling.

What is stored on your device

One value: the name of the county and state you selected, so the app can open on your area next time instead of asking again.

It is stored in the app's own storage on your phone. It is not backed up to us. It is deleted when you tap Change, and when you uninstall the app.

That is the entire list. The app does not store, and has no way to store, who you are, what you looked at, which listings you opened, whether you called anyone, or when you used it.

What the app does not collect

  • No name, email address, phone number, or account of any kind
  • No location. The app never requests location permission. Choosing a county from a list is not the same as the app knowing where you are, and the app does not know where you are.
  • No contacts, photos, microphone, camera, or files
  • No advertising identifier, and no advertising
  • No analytics, crash reporting, session recording, or usage statistics
  • No cookies or tracking of any kind
  • No health information. The app has no way to learn anything about your health, because it never asks you anything.

When the app uses the network

The app is built to work with no signal at all. The whole directory is stored inside the app itself, so it opens and works on a phone in airplane mode, in a basement, or with no data plan.

There are exactly two times the network is involved.

1. Checking for an updated directory. Phone numbers change. When the app opens, it asks Expo's update service whether a newer version of the directory exists, and downloads it if so. That request tells the service which version of the app is asking and which platform it is running on. It does not include anything about you, and it does not include your saved county. Expo's privacy policy is at expo.dev/privacy.

2. When you tap a phone number, a text link, an email address, or a website. The app hands that over to your phone's own dialer, messaging app, email app, or browser, and stops there. The app does not place the call, does not stay on the line, does not record that it happened, and never learns whether you went through with it.

Anyone you contact will see your phone number, the same as any call you make yourself. That is between you and them; it does not pass through this app. If you would rather not show your number, your phone's own settings control that.

Children

The app collects nothing from anyone, including children. It is a public directory of services, some of which serve young people.

If something here is wrong

Report an incorrect or outdated listing to Info@zine01.com.

Every listing shows where its information came from and when it was read. If a listing's details could not be confirmed, the app says so rather than hiding it. If a number in this app is wrong, 988 always works — call or text, 24 hours a day.

In an emergency

This app is a directory, not an emergency service. In immediate danger, call 911. For a suicide or mental health crisis, call or text 988.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever begins collecting anything, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here plainly rather than buried.
